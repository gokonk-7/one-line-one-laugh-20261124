# Variants tested

The judge compares lines two at a time and weighs, most important first:
(1) it is one funny line, whole and self-contained; (2) it actually lands a
laugh — a real turn, surprise or twist, not a mild observation; (3) it is
tight, every word earning its place; (4) it is fresh, not a joke everyone
has heard or a pun obvious from the first word; (5) it is clear on first
read, needing no explanation. Length for its own sake does not count.

288 entries were already in when this was written, so the obvious angles
were assumed taken: AI jokes, programmer jokes, cat jokes, "my code works"
jokes, password jokes, and every pun telegraphed in the first word. The
candidates below were all written fresh in this session and judged against
the brief above.

## Candidates (all under 140 characters)

1. **66 chars — CHOSEN.** "My dentist asked if I floss; I said, 'Only when
   I need an alibi.'" The turn is conceptual, not a pun: floss is reframed
   from dental hygiene into evidence removal, so "alibi" lands as a
   confession of a (tiny, imaginary) crime. Tight — one setup clause, one
   punch clause, no wasted word — and clear on first read. Fresh angle:
   nobody's dentist joke is about needing an alibi.
2. 81 chars. "I asked the barista for something strong; she handed me her
   resignation letter." Vivid scene and a real twist, but the joke runs on
   the double meaning of "strong" (drink vs. strongly worded), which the
   brief counts against: a pun obvious once "strong" is said.
3. 91 chars. "My neighbour's rooster crows at 5 a.m., so I crow back at
   3 a.m. — we have an understanding." Absurd escalation, fresh, but the
   laugh is a smile rather than a laugh, and "we have an understanding"
   needs a beat to land.
4. 101 chars. "My car's check-engine light has been on so long I've
   started treating it as a nightlight." Relatable and visual, but the
   twist is a single mild reframe with no surprise in the final words.
5. 100 chars. "My resume says 'detail-oriented' — I typed it as
   'detial-oriented' and nobody noticed until now." Self-demonstrating
   joke, clever, but risks reading as an actual typo to a fast judge, and
   the laugh depends on spotting the misspelling.
6. 114 chars. "I finally found my purpose in life — unfortunately, it's a
   45-minute drive away and the parking is terrible." Good twist on
   "finding your purpose", but the setup is long for the size of the laugh.

## Second pass

Candidate 1 was revised once: the first draft read "My dentist asked if I
floss; I told him, 'Only when I need an alibi.'" — "I said" replaced
"I told him" (two words saved, no gender assumed, same rhythm), and the
semicolon kept the two clauses in one breath instead of splitting the line
into two sentences.
